The Artifacts We Left Behind and Brought Back

The historic journey to the moon left behind fascinating physical artifacts, both scattered across the lunar surface and carefully preserved in museums back on Earth. These objects serve as powerful historical exhibits and continue to provide valuable scientific data today.

Artifacts on the Lunar Surface

On the surface of the moon, the astronauts left several symbolic and technological items. The most famous artifact is, of course, the flag of the United States, planted as a symbol of the American achievement. Because there is no wind on the moon, the flag was designed with a horizontal rod to keep it extended.

Alongside the flag, the crew left a commemorative plaque attached to the leg of the lunar module descent stage (which remains on the moon forever). The plaque reads: "Here men from the planet Earth first set foot upon the Moon. July 1969, A.D. We came in peace for all mankind." In addition to these symbols, scientific instruments were left behind, such as a laser ranging retroreflector, which is still used by scientists today to measure the exact distance to the moon.

Treasures Brought to Earth

Conversely, the astronauts brought precious artifacts back to Earth. They returned with approximately 21.5 kilograms (47 pounds) of lunar rocks and soil. These samples are considered a scientific treasure and have provided humanity with immense knowledge about the formation of the moon and our solar system. Other physical artifacts that survived the mission are proudly displayed in museums, including the specialized A7L spacesuits that protected the astronauts from extreme temperatures and the vacuum of space, as well as the "Columbia" command module itself.
