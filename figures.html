The People Behind the Historic Mission

While behind the monumental success of the Apollo 11 mission stood an army of approximately 400,000 dedicated engineers, scientists, and technicians, the public faces of this historic endeavor were the three brave astronauts chosen to fly the mission. Each of them played a critical and highly specialized role in ensuring the journey to the unknown was a success.

Meet the Crew

Neil Armstrong - Commander

Serving as the mission commander, Neil Armstrong became the very first human being to step foot on the lunar surface. Before becoming an astronaut, Armstrong was a brilliant test pilot and an aerospace engineer. His exceptional calm under pressure was the key to the landing's success. During the final descent, he had to take manual control of the "Eagle" lunar module, skillfully flying it to a safe landing spot with only seconds of fuel remaining.

Buzz Aldrin - Lunar Module Pilot

Serving as the lunar module pilot, Buzz Aldrin became the second person to walk on the moon. Holding a doctorate in astronautics from MIT, Aldrin was often described as the scientific "brain" of the lunar team. He joined Armstrong on the surface for the historic moonwalk. Together, they spent their time outside the spacecraft collecting lunar soil samples, setting up scientific measurement instruments, and taking iconic photographs.

Michael Collins - Command Module Pilot

While Armstrong and Aldrin descended to the moon's surface, Collins remained alone in the "Columbia" command module, orbiting the moon and waiting for their return. He has often been described as the "loneliest man in history." When his spacecraft passed behind the far side of the moon, he was completely cut off from all radio contact with Earth, carrying the heavy responsibility of bringing them all home safely.
